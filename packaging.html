Concept projects

Five food, drink and wellness packaging concepts

These five concepts use invented brands to show how I approach packaging and product content for food, drink and wellness. They are concept projects, not client launches, and the review steps are illustrative. For each one I wrote the brief and copy, art directed the images with ChatGPT, and built the print dieline, the sample label panel and the product page.

Paloma Pantry

Snack bars | Bar wrapper + 12 count shelf display box

Date sweetened nut bars made in small batches in Los Angeles.

The brief

  • Goal: launch 3 flavors into LA grocery and the brand's own store at the same time.
  • Shelf read: the flavor must read from 6 feet away, so each flavor owns one color.
  • Must fit: Nutrition Facts, allergen line (tree nuts), net weight, UPC and a recycle note on the back.
  • Deliverables: flow wrap for each bar, a 12 count display box that tears open on shelf, and product page images.

How I developed the concept

  1. BriefLaunch 3 flavors into LA grocery and the brand's own store at the same time.
  2. Round 1Two directions. A was a hand drawn market look. B was bold color blocks.
  3. ReviewB read clearer from 6 feet away. A felt warmer. Keep B's color blocks and borrow A's hand drawn fruit.
  4. ApproveRound 2: flavor name moved to the top third so it shows above the shelf lip. Allergen line moved next to the ingredient list.
  5. Print files and pagePrint dieline with bleed, safe area and legal panel, then the product page

Tidewell

Functional sparkling water | 12 oz slim can + 4 pack carrier

Sparkling water with magnesium and electrolytes, made for long LA beach days.

The brief

  • Goal: stand out in a crowded cold case of sparkling water cans.
  • Must fit: Nutrition Facts, CA cash refund value mark, the flavor and 'magnesium + electrolytes' in plain words. No health claims beyond the label rules.
  • Print limits: printed aluminum cans, so the art is built for a few spot colors plus white.
  • Deliverables: 3 can wraps, a 4 pack paperboard carrier and product page images.

How I developed the concept

  1. BriefStand out in a crowded cold case of sparkling water cans.
  2. Round 1Two directions. A used a full wave illustration. B was a clean horizon line.
  3. ReviewA looked busy in a cold case photo. B read fast. Keep B and bring in a small wave from A as the flavor color band.
  4. ApproveRound 2: dropped one ink to fit the can printer's color limit. The flavor band moved up to sit above the hand grip.
  5. Print files and pagePrint dieline with bleed, safe area and legal panel, then the product page

Hush Hollow

Evening calm gummies | 60 count jar label + outer box

Evening gummies with magnesium and chamomile for winding down.

The brief

  • Goal: a calm, grown up supplement look that does not feel like candy.
  • Must fit: Supplement Facts panel, serving size, the required 'This statement has not been evaluated by the FDA' footnote, lot and best by area.
  • Shelf read: the jar must face forward with the brand name and count visible at a glance.
  • Deliverables: jar wrap label, a tuck end outer box and product page images.

How I developed the concept

  1. BriefA calm, grown up supplement look that does not feel like candy.
  2. Round 1Two directions. A was a night sky full of stars. B was one simple moon over a hollow.
  3. ReviewA felt like a kids product. B felt calm and premium. Keep B.
  4. ApproveRound 2: the gummy count got its own badge. The Supplement Facts panel moved to the side so it does not wrap out of view.
  5. Print files and pagePrint dieline with bleed, safe area and legal panel, then the product page

Oro Canyon

Clean body care | Bottle and tube labels + gift box

Plant based body care inspired by the golden hills around LA.

The brief

  • Goal: a body care set that looks good on a bathroom shelf and as a gift.
  • Must fit: full ingredient list (INCI names), net contents in oz and mL, the period after opening symbol and a made in USA line.
  • Label stock: clear and kraft label tests, because the wash bottle is amber and the oil is glass.
  • Deliverables: 3 labels sized to each container, a gift box and product page images.

How I developed the concept

  1. BriefA body care set that looks good on a bathroom shelf and as a gift.
  2. Round 1Two directions. A was a landscape photo label. B was a simple sun arch mark.
  3. ReviewThe photo got muddy on the amber bottle. B stayed clean on every container. Keep B.
  4. ApproveRound 2: product type moved above the scent name so shoppers know what it is first. The ingredient text was resized to stay readable.
  5. Print files and pagePrint dieline with bleed, safe area and legal panel, then the product page

Ember & Elm

Small batch coffee | 12 oz stand up pouch + roast stickers

Small batch coffee roasted in LA, in a pouch built to change roasts without new printing.

The brief

  • Goal: one printed pouch for every roast, so new coffees launch fast with no new print run.
  • System: a printed base pouch plus a roast sticker for the name, tasting notes and roast level.
  • Must fit: net weight, roast date box, one way valve spot and a recycle note.
  • Deliverables: base pouch art, 3 roast stickers on one sticker sheet and product page images.

How I developed the concept

  1. BriefOne printed pouch for every roast, so new coffees launch fast with no new print run.
  2. Round 1Two directions. A printed every roast as its own bag. B used one base bag plus stickers.
  3. ReviewB saves a print run for each new coffee and keeps the shelf looking like one brand. Keep B.
  4. ApproveRound 2: the roast date box moved off the valve area. Sticker size locked to one standard size from the label printer.
  5. Print files and pagePrint dieline with bleed, safe area and legal panel, then the product page
Creative operations

How I run creative ops

I organize briefs, approvals, print files and product page updates so a small team can keep work moving and deadlines stay visible.

Team leadership

I have led the people doing the work

As Content Strategy Lead at Media Scaling, I led content for up to 12 creator and brand accounts at once, each with its own pod of editors, social media managers and scriptwriters. At KBF Home Improvement I led the outsourced VA team, then built AI systems to automate many of its repeatable workflows.

My approach for a content manager and two designers: a clear brief for every job, one board where everyone sees what is due, and written feedback within a day.

1 | Briefs

One page, everything the designer needs

Every job starts with a short brief, so nobody designs from a guess. Here is the brief for the Tidewell can.

Goal
Stand out in a crowded cold case
Must say
Flavor, 12 FL OZ, magnesium + electrolytes
Legal
Nutrition Facts, CA refund value, no health claims
Specs
12 oz slim can, 4 spot colors + white
Due
Round 1 in 3 days, print files in 2 weeks
Sign off
Brand lead, then regulatory, then printer proof
2 | Approvals

Every file passes the same steps

  1. Request
  2. Brief
  3. Round 1
  4. Review
  5. Round 2
  6. Approve
  7. Printer proof
  8. Release

Brand, regulatory copy and the printer each check what they own. Notes come back in writing, in one place, with one person making the final call. Nothing goes to print without a signed proof.

3 | Tracking

One board shows what is due

I use Notion and ClickUp to track every deliverable with its owner, due date, approval status and proof. The same workflow sets up in Wrike with request forms, custom statuses and proofing.

At Media Scaling I built a shared tracker for daily deadline checks and a weekly scorecard covering about 18 editors and 7 social media managers. Read the case study →

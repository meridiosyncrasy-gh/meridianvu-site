The big idea

Most apps were doing one small job

Paid Shopify apps pile up over time, and they keep billing even when the store only uses a small part of them.

On the live store, I found that most apps handled one or two small jobs I could recreate with Shopify's built-in tools or theme code. I rebuilt and tested those features, then cancelled the apps.

The problem

12 paid apps at $641.93 a month

AV Figures runs on Shopify Advanced. Its November 15 Shopify bill was $2,691, and $388.13 of that was app charges. Tools billed outside Shopify added more, like a $255 a month email pop-up tool. Added up at their monthly list prices, the 12 paid apps came to $641.93 a month.

The owner wanted to cut the bill without breaking anything customers rely on: custom decal orders with dozens of options, pre-order rules, sale prices, members-only products and a weekly newsletter. Some apps also held years of settings and data that nobody had written down.

How I did it

Five steps, one app at a time

1. Audit

Read every app charge from the Shopify bill and outside invoices, then listed what each app was really doing on the live store.

2. Rank

Ordered all 12 apps from easiest and lowest risk to hardest, and agreed the order with the owner before touching anything.

3. Back up, rebuild, test

Recorded each app's live settings, backed them up, rebuilt the feature with Shopify Flow, Forms, Messaging, product fields or theme code, and tested it on a copy of the theme before going live.

4. Cancel before billing

Uninstalled each app and cleaned up its leftover code, timed before the next charge where possible. Three apps were cancelled ahead of their next charge.

5. Document

Wrote every change into one running audit doc, so the owner can change limits, options and rules later without the app or me.

Trade-offs

What changed for the store

Not every replacement works exactly like the app it replaced. I wrote each difference down for the owner before going live.

Fraud orders

The app blocked bad buyers at checkout. With Flow, the order goes through and the card is charged, then the order is cancelled and refunded. Blocking visitors by VPN or IP address has no free Shopify version.

Hidden products

The old app used secret link tokens. Unlisted products stay out of search and collections, but open for anyone with the plain product link.

File uploads

The upload limit dropped from 100 MB to 20 MB. The largest file a customer had ever sent was 11 MB.

Support email

Replies sent as the store's own address from a free Gmail inbox were missing email signing (DKIM), and one email provider rejected them. Outgoing support mail now goes through a low-cost signing mail service. AI-drafted support replies are still being set up.

Paid membership

Moving members without making them sign up again depends on saved payment IDs that the app maker has not exported yet. I wrote up the options for the owner.

The result

What it added up to

  • 11 of 12 paid apps replaced or dropped, and uninstalled, in 2 days, November 23 to 24, 2026.
  • $562.93 a month in app fees cut, plus usage fees from two apps. That is about $6,755 a year.
  • After the main new cost, Shopify Messaging usage at about $130 a month, the store saves about $433 a month, or about $5,200 a year projected.
  • 526 sale variants, 19,968 support tickets and every app's settings backed up before anything was removed.
  • Every rule, limit and option I rebuilt now lives in the store, with a written guide for changing it later.

1

Career Goal Mapping

Figure out what you want, and write it down once so every other skill can use it.

video coming soonWalkthrough of Career Goal Mapping

Claude interviews you a few questions at a time about where you are now and where you want to go. Then it builds your Career Profile: your job tracks, the lowest pay you can live on, your yes and no lists, and a "proof bank" of your real wins with real numbers. Every other skill in this set reads this profile.

Before you start

Your current resume (any version), any old cover letters or portfolio links, and a rough idea of the pay you need.

What you'll have after

A Career Profile doc saved in your Claude Project, plus an application log to track every job you go for.

Notes: how to run it
  • Say: "map my career goals" or "build my career profile."
  • Answer in your own words. It uses how you talk to write your applications later.
  • It will point out things that don't add up, like a dream job that pays under your floor. That's a good thing.
  • Come back every few months, or after a big win, and say "add this win to my proof bank."

Next: Move on to Resume Builder once your profile is saved.

2

Resume Builder

Turn your proof bank into a clean resume that passes the robot check and reads well.

video coming soonWalkthrough of Resume Builder

It checks your current resume for problems first: numbers that don't match, skills you're still learning listed as mastered, and layouts that confuse the software companies use to scan resumes (called an ATS). Then it builds a master resume plus one version for each job track, and exports clean PDFs.

Before you start

Your Career Profile from step 1, and your current resume files.

What you'll have after

A master resume, one resume per track, and PDFs ready to upload. It can also tailor a copy to one specific job post.

Notes: how to run it
  • Say: "update my resume" or "make a resume for [type of role]."
  • It shows the resume as a page you can look at first. Ask for changes until you love it.
  • To tailor one, paste the job post and say "tailor my resume to this job."
  • It never adds a skill or number that isn't in your proof bank.

Next: Move on to Portfolio Builder. If your work already has strong proof online, you can skip ahead to Job Screening.

3

Portfolio Builder

Show your work, so employers can see it and not just read about it.

video coming soonWalkthrough of Portfolio Builder

It looks at the work you already have and maps it to your job tracks. Then it writes case studies (short stories about a project and its results) and can build you a free portfolio website. If a track has no proof yet, it helps you plan a "spec project": a made up brand or concept you create to show the skill.

Before you start

Your Career Profile, plus links or files of past work: old portfolios, screenshots, live sites, videos.

What you'll have after

Case studies, a portfolio site or pages, and PDFs sized for job forms.

Notes: how to run it
  • Say: "build my portfolio" or "write a case study for [project]."
  • No proof for a role you want? Say "I have no proof for this kind of role" and it will plan a spec project.
  • It keeps you honest: spec projects are always labeled as concepts, never as paid client work.

Next: Move on to Job Screening to start finding jobs worth your time.

4

Job Screening

Stop wasting time on jobs that were never a good fit.

video coming soonWalkthrough of Job Screening

Paste a job link, or ask it to search for you. For each job it writes a short screening card: is it really remote, what the company does, how it fits your tracks, how the pay compares to your floor, a safe pay number to put on the form, red flags, and which resume and portfolio piece to use. Then it gives a clear call: Apply, Maybe or Skip.

Before you start

Your Career Profile, and a job link or the text of a job post. Connecting Indeed in Claude helps but isn't required.

What you'll have after

A screening card for every job, and a ranked list of the ones worth applying to.

Notes: how to run it
  • Say: "is this job a good fit?" and paste the link.
  • Or say: "run my daily job search."
  • Asking "what salary should I put?" gets you a number based on the posted range or the company research.

Next: Send your Apply list to Application Writer.

5

Application Writer

Cover letters and form answers that sound like you, not like a robot.

video coming soonWalkthrough of Application Writer

It writes cover letters, answers to application questions and short recruiter messages in your own voice, using your real wins. It can also fill out the application in your browser. By default it uses Review mode: it fills everything in and stops, so you read it and click submit yourself. Every application goes in your log so you never apply to the same job twice.

Before you start

Your Career Profile, your resume and portfolio from steps 2 and 3, and a job marked Apply from step 4.

What you'll have after

A cover letter (doc and PDF), saved answers, a filled application, and an updated application log.

Notes: how to run it
  • Say: "write a cover letter for this job" or "apply to this job."
  • Stay in Review mode for your first 10 or so applications. Switch to Auto mode only once the letters sound right to you.
  • It will never type a password, make an account for you, or answer the voluntary demographic questions.
  • Ask "what have I applied to?" anytime for a quick summary.

Next: When you hear back, use Interview Prep and Follow Up.

6

Interview Prep and Follow Up

Walk in ready, talk pay with confidence, and follow up the right way.

video coming soonWalkthrough of Interview Prep and Follow Up

It researches the company and builds a one page cheat sheet for your call: their own words to use, your best stories matched to what they care about, questions to ask, and your pay numbers. It also writes STAR answers, a 30-60-90 day plan, pay talk scripts, and every follow up email you'll need. It drafts emails but never sends one without your yes.

Before you start

Your Career Profile, the job's row in your application log, and the interview details (date, time, who you're meeting).

What you'll have after

A research pack, a cheat sheet, practice answers, and ready to send thank you and follow up emails.

Notes: how to run it
  • Say: "I got an interview with [company], prep me."
  • Ask for a mock interview. It asks one question at a time and gives you feedback after each answer.
  • Say: "write a thank you email" the same day as your interview.
  • Gone quiet? Say "they ghosted me" and it writes a friendly check in.

Next: That's the full loop! Every new win goes back into your Career Profile, so each round gets easier.

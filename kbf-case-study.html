The big idea

What changed

KBF sells cabinets, vanities, countertops, flooring and hardware from dozens of vendors. A store like that lives or dies on its data: thousands of products, prices that change every season, and items that get discontinued without warning.

I took every repeat data job in the business and turned it into a system that runs on a schedule, checks its own work and tells a person when something needs a decision.

The problem

Where the time went

Every vendor sends price lists in its own format. Prices change, items go out of stock, and new product lines show up in the inbox. For four years, this was handled by an outsourced team of virtual assistants on Upwork (31 contracts since 2022) typing products and prices in by hand.

At the busiest point, that was roughly 150 VA hours a week, plus up to 60 hours a week of my own time checking and fixing their work. Mistakes slipped through, like combo prices nobody trusted, and the team cost up to $43K a year. The catalog wanted to grow faster than people could type.

What I built

The systems

Price list pipeline

Weekly scan, weekday imports

A Saturday scan finds new price lists in the sales inbox and adds them to a queue. A weekday run imports the ready ones into Notion and Shopify. A noon task follows up with vendors when cost info is missing, then turns itself off when they answer. It never deletes a price row, never guesses a discount, and checks every import with counts and spot checks against the vendor file.

Weekly web dev report

Every Saturday

Scans the inbox for new vendor accounts and price lists, gives each vendor a code, adds them to the vendor roster in Notion and writes that week's report, carrying over anything still open.

Bulk product uploads

On request

Skills that turn a vendor file into finished Shopify products through the Shopify Admin API: titles, tags, variants, SEO fields and images, in the store's own naming rules.

Photo fixer

One checked run

Made 13,307 product photos square across 3,155 products in one run, then confirmed zero were left out of shape.

Blog and Pinterest system

Daily

Moved the blog from a paid app to Shopify's own blog. One SEO guide goes live each day and two pins a day are planned and logged in Notion.

App cleanup

October 2026

Cut 8 paid or unused Shopify apps and rebuilt the features they handled straight into the theme.

How it started

2022 to now

  • 2022: The store opensI built the KBF Home store on Shopify from scratch and set up a ClickUp hub to track every task and owner.
  • Guides for a remote teamStep by step guides so VAs could bulk upload products by CSV, format descriptions the same way every time and catch mistakes before they went live.
  • ChatGPT drafts, people reviewA ChatGPT project loaded with the brand story, keyword rules and one gold standard description. VAs made drafts, then checked them against my revision checklist.
  • The store grows upA visual mega menu, filters for size, color and material, and a catalog spread across 85 collections.
  • Renders and daily contentAn AI render pipeline with 300+ renders so far, and a short-form system for 2 to 3 posts a day.
  • 2026: The guides become Claude skillsThe same guides now run as Claude skills that add products, sync vendor price lists and write the blog. 10,871 products went live in 8 weeks and the store passed 15,000 active products.

I built the system for people first. Then I taught it to AI.

AI renders

300+ rooms and product shots

Most vendors only send a cutout photo. So I built an image pipeline with ComfyUI, Gemini and Photoshop that places each cabinet, vanity and stone slab in a styled room. Every countertop gets a kitchen, a bathroom and a flat lay.

Short-form

Daily content, as a system

Five-second minis on top, and pick-a-style carousels below that play through every slide. I set up the formats and the guides behind them.

Ads

Animated ads, built to match

Paid social ads for kitchen remodels, countertop slabs and the Industry Pro Program, shown the way people see them: stories full screen, and square ads in the Instagram feed.

Showroom

Showroom window clings

The same look carries into the store: each poster is printed as a window cling that fits one glass bay of the showroom front.

Store design

Browsing made easy

A visual mega menu so shoppers see the category before they click, with filters built on product tags and fields. Tap a tab to open each menu.

The result

What it added up to

  • About 190 work hours a week saved and up to $43K a year in labor costs cut, with the work now running on systems.
  • 10,871 products added in the 8 weeks after the VA team ended, close to the 11,951 they added over about 4 years.
  • The catalog passed 15,000 active products across 85 collections.
  • 61 vendor price lists fully synced, with 72 vendors connected to price tracking.
  • 58 SEO guides live on the store blog.

A person still reviews imports, answers vendor questions and approves anything the system flags. The system does the typing and the checking, so one operator runs what used to take a team.

For you

What this proves

If your team spends its week moving data between inboxes, spreadsheets and your store, that work can be a system. I map how it runs today, build it with tools you already pay for, write a how-to guide for every step, and keep a person in charge of the decisions.

The team you have can do far more once systems handle the repeat work.
